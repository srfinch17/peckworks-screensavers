# Cotswold Brook: retired

Retired 2026-10-09. The code stays as a reference for what not to do; `scripts\publish.ps1`
skips any folder holding a `RETIRED.md`, and it is out of `PeckworksScreensavers.slnx`, so it
is no longer built, checked, or kept in step with the engine (it may stop compiling as the
engine changes).

The owner's verdict, after watching it: "a fetid pile of hot garbage". The fish were "just
stupid overlays that wiggled in place, then slid around. They were oriented wrong, looked
terrible, and behaved exactly how fish don't."

Photo by [Martin Fenton](https://unsplash.com/@mhfphotographygb) on
[Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/photos/9BFnbb1sfZo), used under the Unsplash License.

## What went wrong

1. **The fish were stickers.** One flat, side-on picture of a trout, flipped like a playing card
   to turn round, able to point only left or right. The camera looks ACROSS the water from the
   bank, so a real fish would be seen from above and at a slant, foreshortened, pointing any
   way on the water's surface. "Darting" slid the picture sideways; a real trout bends its whole
   body into a turn, bursts forward with a few hard tail beats and glides.
2. **The fish should not have been there at all.** The pond is a still golden mirror. From the
   bank, a reflection that bright hides anything under it: you see the sky and the houses, not
   the fish. The rule "dark fish only over bright water" was a patch that made an impossible
   thing a little more visible instead of asking whether it belonged.
3. **Nothing that moves was ever judged moving.** Every check was a still frame or a frame
   time. The owner judges by watching, and so must the builder: a still cannot show a slide, a
   flip or a wiggle in place.
4. **"Much better" was read as approval of every part.** It was said about the first build next
   to a far worse one (Cabin by Stream). Two more sessions added visible fish, a smoother dusk, a
   depth map, fireflies among the reeds and mist on the water, on top of a saver whose most
   visible moving thing was its worst, without anyone watching it for a few minutes first.
5. **A real photo raises the bar for everything that moves on it.** A cartoon fish in a painted
   scene matches its world; the same fish on a photograph is the one thing the eye goes to.

## The rules that came out of it (also in the repo's working notes)

- A creature on a photo must be right on all four counts, or it is left out: it is VISIBLE there
  in reality (glare, depth, light), it is drawn in the photo's own perspective (any heading on
  its ground or water plane, foreshortened by the camera's angle), it turns by bending through a
  curve and never by flipping, and it moves the way that animal moves (for a trout: holds facing
  the current, small tail beats, burst and glide; never slides sideways). Absence beats a fake.
- Before showing anything that moves, watch it: dump 10 to 20 seconds of frames
  (`PECKWORKS_FRAMES`), look at them as a strip or a GIF, and compare with real footage of the
  real thing. Judge each moving element on its own.
- Before adding to an existing saver, watch the whole of it in motion for a few minutes and name
  its weakest moving element first. Do not polish around a rotten core.
